## 1. Mission

> “Warsha finishes your work safely, for the fairest price.”

### 1.1 “Finishes your work”

Warsha exists to help a customer progress from a real service need to a clear agreement with an eligible independent worker, through performance, completion, payment, follow-up, and fair resolution when something goes wrong. Discovery alone is not the outcome. The marketplace must support accountable completion without representing workers as Warsha employees.

### 1.2 “Safely”

**LOCKED:** Safety primarily means safe and transparent marketplace participation. It includes:

- safe and transparent transactions;
- protection against rip-offs and coercive underpayment;
- verified identities;
- clear agreed pricing and scope;
- no unauthorized price changes;
- secure payment foundations;
- fair dispute handling;
- accountability for customers and workers;
- privacy-preserving communication; and
- evidence-based operational decisions.

Physical safety remains important, but the word “safely” must never be reduced to physical safety alone.

### 1.3 “Fairest price”

**LOCKED:** The fairest price is neither automatically the cheapest nor automatically the most expensive. It is fair relative to:

- the agreed work scope;
- worker skill, reliability, and service quality;
- local market conditions;
- materials, urgency, travel, warranty, and job complexity where relevant; and
- the legitimate interests of both the customer and the independent worker.

Customers remain free to prefer a cheaper quote, a balanced-value quote, or a higher-priced quote. Warsha may explain value, but it must not make every choice converge on the lowest price.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Worker

An independent craftsman or service provider who may use Warsha as an optional source of additional jobs. “Worker” is the preferred product-facing term. Established internal code may temporarily use “provider” where a safe migration has not yet occurred.

### 2.2 Customer

A person seeking household or related services and entering agreements with independent workers through Warsha.

### 2.3 Marketplace

The Warsha system that supports discovery, eligibility, matching, quoting, agreement, booking, communication, payment foundations, evidence, review, and resolution.

### 2.4 Agreement

The clear, recorded scope, worker, price, timing, inclusions, exclusions, payment method, and applicable warranty terms accepted by both parties before work begins.

### 2.5 Best Value

A balanced assessment of price, quality, reliability, timing, distance, completeness, warranty, and prior relationship. Best Value is not a synonym for cheapest, highest rated, closest, fastest, or most experienced.

### 2.6 Inference

A conclusion derived from authorized, relevant marketplace behavior. Inference may reduce configuration burden, but it must not weaken security, privacy, legal compliance, or a person’s ability to understand and challenge consequential decisions.

### 2.7 Product labels

- **LOCKED:** Binding and changeable only through constitutional or specification change control.
- **DEFERRED:** Intentionally undecided until stated evidence and a review trigger exist.
- **PROPOSED:** An engineering or product baseline that is not yet locked.
- **FUTURE:** Outside the current launch commitment and additive to the independent-worker marketplace.

## 3. Non-negotiable principles

### 3.1 Independent workers first

**LOCKED:** Warsha is primarily built for independent craftsmen and workers, including:

- plumbers;
- electricians;
- carpenters;
- AC technicians;
- cleaners;
- painters;
- appliance repair workers;
- satellite and TV technicians;
- moving helpers; and
- general maintenance workers.

Businesses may use Warsha, but large-company, team, dispatcher, and enterprise workflows must not shape the MVP worker experience.

**FUTURE:** Warsha B2C, Warsha Business, Warsha B2B, small-business teams, and enterprise maintenance may be additive products. They must not displace or complicate the independent-worker core.

Warsha does not hire workers, employ them, manage their careers, require Warsha to be their primary income source, or promise work. Product language must not imply employment.

### 3.2 Simplicity

**LOCKED:** Worker experiences must assume that some workers have limited formal education or technical literacy, dislike long instructions, avoid configuration, and mainly want to receive and complete work.

Worker experiences therefore prioritize:

- large, clear actions;
- minimal reading;
- minimal configuration;
- job-first navigation;
- automatic inference where safe;
- natural Egyptian Arabic;
- no enterprise dashboards;
- no unnecessary metrics;
- no gamification;
- no academy or training system;
- no career ladder;
- no badge zoo; and
- no forced email requirement.

Phone verification is the primary worker account contact method. Email remains optional unless a future legal or technical requirement is documented and approved through change control.

### 3.3 Infer instead of configure

**LOCKED:** Warsha should infer information from authorized real behavior whenever it can do so safely. Examples include historical pricing from completed jobs, capacity from accepted bookings and duration, reliability from booking behavior, customer preferences from actual selections, and opportunity fairness from invitation history.

Worker-facing availability remains one simple choice: Available or Unavailable.

Inference must not replace required identity, security, consent, legal, financial, or safety controls.

### 3.4 Best value over extremes

**LOCKED:** Warsha must not optimize solely for lowest price, highest rating, shortest distance, fastest response, or most experience. Price is especially important in Egypt, and customers must be able to sort by and select lower-priced quotes. Customers must also be able to choose higher-priced workers. Higher historical pricing is not automatically bad.

### 3.5 Protect both parties

**LOCKED:** Customers and workers are both entitled to transparent agreements, privacy, fair cancellation and dispute processes, evidence submission, protection against abuse, no hidden financial deductions, and no arbitrary punishment for an isolated incident. Patterns, rates, context, and recent trends matter more than one-off mistakes.

### 3.6 No rip-off policy

**LOCKED:** Warsha reduces overcharging risk through competing quotes, historical pricing intelligence, transparent price revisions, explicit customer approval, clear inclusions and exclusions, receipts, dispute evidence, and prohibition of silent final-price replacement.

Warsha also protects workers from underpayment, scope pressure, and unfair work demands.

### 3.7 Transaction integrity

**LOCKED:** A customer and worker form a clear agreement before work begins. After a quote is selected and the worker confirms:

- the customer is committed to that worker, subject to fair cancellation and dispute rules;
- a later competing quote cannot replace the selection;
- a price change requires explicit customer approval; and
- a material scope change requires a new request where appropriate.

## 4. Worker principles

Workers must receive understandable job information, clear pricing and payment terms, privacy protection, a fair opportunity to respond, and an honest explanation of marketplace outcomes.

Eligibility and ranking may protect customer outcomes, but:

- a failed hard eligibility or safety rule cannot be repaired by ranking;
- a new verified worker receives controlled opportunity to establish a record;
- a worker is not punished arbitrarily for one cancellation, decline, missed invitation, or complaint;
- declining before an agreement is not misconduct;
- opportunity adjustments recover over time;
- internal scores, competitor quotes, customer trust data, and exact ranking weights are not exposed; and
- no hidden deduction may be made from worker earnings.

Workers may choose Available or Unavailable. They must not be required to maintain weekly schedules, lunch breaks, vacation planners, or shift calendars for marketplace eligibility.

## 5. Customer principles

Customers must be able to:

- understand whether a displayed price is historical, estimated, quoted, revised, or final;
- compare meaningful alternatives without being forced toward the cheapest;
- choose lower-priced or higher-priced quotes intentionally;
- see inclusions, exclusions, timing, payment methods, and applicable warranty before agreement;
- cancel freely before selecting a worker;
- approve or reject price revisions explicitly;
- recover from request expiry without blame;
- receive clear updates when matching, selection, confirmation, cancellation, rescue, or delay changes the outcome;
- submit relevant evidence for no-shows, warranty issues, and disputes; and
- access a fair process when work or payment goes wrong.

Customer outcomes come first, while long-term marketplace health remains essential to sustaining those outcomes.

## 6. Marketplace principles

### 6.1 Eligibility before ranking

Every applicable service, distance, availability, account, verification, payment, emergency, credential, block, and safety rule is a hard gate. Ranking cannot override an ineligible result.

### 6.2 Healthy competition

Warsha seeks useful choice, not unlimited volume. Requests are not broadcast to every worker. Invitations may expand progressively, while already invited workers retain a fair chance to respond until the request closes or expires.

### 6.3 Fair opportunity

No worker should permanently dominate because early jobs produced early reviews. New verified workers deserve a modest, controlled opportunity to establish themselves. Fairness must not override safety, eligibility, quality floors, or clearly superior customer outcomes.

### 6.4 Evidence over labels

Marketplace intelligence uses relevant behavior and robust statistics. It must not publicly label a worker “Budget,” “Mid-market,” or “Premium,” publicly label customer price preferences, or claim artificial intelligence when rules are deterministic.

### 6.5 Data-driven evolution

**LOCKED:** A decision dependent on real customer or marketplace behavior is deferred rather than guessed. Every deferred decision records:

- why it is deferred;
- the data required to decide it; and
- the event or threshold that triggers review.

## 7. Financial principles

Financial behavior must preserve:

- clear agreed prices and immutable approved price history;
- explicit approval for every price revision;
- no payment against an unapproved amount;
- no hidden customer fees or worker deductions;
- accurate receipts and evidence;
- server-authoritative financial state;
- secure, provider-neutral foundations;
- fair handling of cash commission debt;
- no fictional money movement; and
- auditable refunds, disputes, adjustments, and releases.

**LOCKED:** The WPS-007 financial rules remain authoritative within their scope. Marketplace Intelligence must integrate with them rather than duplicate or bypass them. A marketplace ranking or rescue action must never alter approved money, create a payment, release earnings, or impose a deduction outside those rules.

## 8. Trust and privacy principles

### 8.1 Identity and accountability

Workers must pass the applicable identity and credential rules before receiving eligible marketplace work. Customers and workers remain accountable for abuse, but one unverified signal does not justify automatic punishment.

### 8.2 Communication privacy

**LOCKED:** Personal phone numbers should not be exposed as part of the intended long-term product. Future calling uses number masking or relay infrastructure.

Chat is booking-scoped. Cancelled-booking chat becomes read-only immediately. Completed-booking chat may remain writable only for the currently intended 48-hour follow-up period, after which it becomes read-only.

### 8.3 Location privacy

Exact customer location is shared only when necessary for the agreed service. Matching and preselection experiences should use the least precise location that still permits eligibility and useful quoting. Raw location history must not be exposed or retained without a justified purpose.

### 8.4 Internal intelligence

Matching scores, score components, candidate pools, customer trust signals, worker opportunity state, fraud signals, and competitor quote information are restricted. Customer- and worker-facing outcomes reveal only what is necessary to understand and act.

### 8.5 Evidence and decisions

No-show, dispute, cancellation, emergency-misuse, and abuse decisions should rely on authorized evidence, context, rates, and patterns. Evidence access and retention must be purpose-limited and auditable.

## 9. Product decision hierarchy

The binding hierarchy is:

1. **Warsha Constitution**
2. **Warsha Product Specification (WPS)**
3. **Warsha Engineering Specification (WES)**
4. Implementation, operations, tests, analytics definitions, and experiments

When two documents conflict, the higher document prevails. Silence in a higher document does not authorize a lower document to invent material product behavior. A material unresolved choice must be documented and escalated.

Established implementation terminology may remain temporarily where renaming creates unnecessary risk, but product-facing terminology and behavior must follow the governing documents.

## 10. Change-control rules

### 10.1 Constitutional changes

A constitutional change requires:

- an explicit version increment;
- owner approval;
- the reason for the change;
- an impact assessment covering customers, workers, marketplace health, finance, trust, privacy, accessibility, and existing agreements;
- identification of every affected WPS, WES, implementation, test, and operational process; and
- a dated changelog entry.

No silent constitutional change is permitted.

### 10.2 Specification changes

A WPS may clarify product behavior only within this Constitution. A WES may specify implementation only within both the Constitution and its WPS. Any conflict is resolved upward, never by implementation convenience.

### 10.3 Deferred decisions

A deferred decision may be resolved only when its required data exists and its review trigger occurs, or when the owner explicitly authorizes earlier review. The resolution must be documented before implementation changes product behavior.

### 10.4 Emergency engineering changes

A security or operational emergency may temporarily disable unsafe behavior. It may not silently create new commercial, employment, privacy, or fairness policy. The temporary control, reason, owner, scope, and expiry must be documented.

## 11. Constitutional acceptance checklist

Every significant product or engineering change must answer “yes” to all applicable items:

- [ ] Does it help Warsha finish work safely and at a fair price?
- [ ] Does “safely” include transaction, identity, pricing, payment, dispute, accountability, privacy, and evidence concerns?
- [ ] Does it preserve independent-worker status and avoid employment language?
- [ ] Is the worker experience simple, job-first, and usable in natural Egyptian Arabic?
- [ ] Is worker email optional and phone verification primary?
- [ ] Does it infer safe operational information instead of requiring complex worker configuration?
- [ ] Does it preserve a single Available/Unavailable worker control?
- [ ] Does it optimize for Best Value instead of a single extreme?
- [ ] Can customers intentionally choose cheaper or more expensive quotes?
- [ ] Are both parties protected from abuse, hidden changes, and arbitrary punishment?
- [ ] Are hard eligibility and safety checks applied before ranking or fairness?
- [ ] Can new verified workers receive controlled opportunity without weakening customer outcomes?
- [ ] Are pricing agreements and revisions explicit, approved, and auditable?
- [ ] Are WPS-007 financial controls reused rather than bypassed?
- [ ] Are exact locations, phone numbers, trust data, scores, and competitor data protected?
- [ ] Does cancelled chat become read-only immediately and completed chat after the approved follow-up window?
- [ ] Are consequential decisions based on evidence, context, and patterns?
- [ ] Are behavior-dependent unknowns marked DEFERRED with reason, data, and trigger?
- [ ] Does the change comply with the Constitution → WPS → WES hierarchy?
- [ ] Are required document versions, approvals, impact analysis, and changelog entries complete?
